Foods high in lycopene

A red plant pigment and antioxidant, richest in cooked tomatoes. The richest common sources per serving are tomato juice (canned), marinara sauce and tomato paste.

What lycopene does

Lycopene is the carotenoid that colours tomatoes, watermelon, pink grapefruit and guava red. It’s an antioxidant, and diets rich in it are linked with healthier hearts and a lower risk of some cancers. Unlike beta-carotene, your body doesn’t turn it into vitamin A.

How much you need

No set amount. Studies link around 10 mg a day or more with health benefits

Tips

  • Cooking tomatoes and eating them with a little oil makes far more of their lycopene available: tomato paste and sauce beat raw tomatoes.

What blocks it

  • Meals with no fat at all: Lycopene dissolves in fat. A little olive oil with tomatoes helps you absorb much more of it.

Good to know

  • Tomato paste has many times the lycopene of fresh tomatoes, gram for gram.
