Foods high in vitamin K (phylloquinone)

Needed for blood clotting and healthy bones. The richest common sources per serving are mustard greens (cooked), spinach (cooked) and dandelion greens. The Daily Value is 120 µg a day for adults.

What vitamin K (phylloquinone) does

Vitamin K switches on the proteins that make your blood clot when you’re cut, and others that help bind calcium into your bones.

How much you need

The Daily Value is 120 µg a day for adults.

Tips

  • Leafy greens are the top source. Eat them with some fat to absorb it.
  • If you take a blood thinner such as warfarin, keep your vitamin K intake steady from day to day and talk to your doctor before changing your diet.

What blocks it

  • Meals with no fat at all: The vitamin K in leafy greens is absorbed far better with a little oil or other fat.
  • High-dose vitamin E supplements: Large doses work against vitamin K and can raise the risk of bleeding.

Good to know

  • Newborns get a vitamin K shot at birth, because they’re born with very little.
  • The "K" comes from "Koagulation", German for clotting.
  • Bacteria in fermented foods such as natto and cheese make another form, vitamin K2.
