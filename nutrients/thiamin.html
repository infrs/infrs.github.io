Foods high in vitamin B1 (thiamin)

Helps turn carbohydrates into energy and keeps nerves working. The richest common sources per serving are corn flakes (fortified), pork tenderloin (cooked) and canned tomatoes. The Daily Value is 1.2 mg a day for adults.

What vitamin B1 (thiamin) does

Thiamin helps the enzymes that turn carbohydrates into energy. That matters most for your brain and nerves, which run mainly on glucose, and for your heart and muscles.

How much you need

The Daily Value is 1.2 mg a day for adults.

Tips

  • Whole grains, pork, legumes, seeds and enriched bread or pasta are good sources.
  • It dissolves in water and is damaged by long cooking, so steaming keeps more than boiling.
  • Heavy drinking blocks thiamin absorption, so people who drink a lot often run low.

What blocks it

  • Tea and coffee: Compounds in them change thiamin into a form your body can’t use.
  • Raw fish and shellfish: They contain thiaminase, an enzyme that breaks thiamin down. Cooking destroys the enzyme.
  • Alcohol: It blocks thiamin from being absorbed and stored.

Good to know

  • Your body stores only a few weeks’ worth, so you need it regularly.
  • Beriberi, the thiamin deficiency disease, spread in parts of Asia when polished white rice replaced brown rice.
