Foods high in vitamin C (ascorbic acid)

Needed for collagen, wound healing and immunity, and boosts iron absorption. The richest common sources per serving are acerola cherries, guava and blackcurrants. The Daily Value is 90 mg a day for adults.

What vitamin C (ascorbic acid) does

Vitamin C is needed to make collagen, the protein that holds together skin, blood vessels, bones and gums, so it’s essential for healing wounds. It’s also an antioxidant, supports your immune system, and greatly increases how much iron you absorb from plant foods.

How much you need

The Daily Value is 90 mg a day for adults.

Tips

  • Heat, air and long storage destroy it, so raw or lightly cooked fruit and vegetables keep the most.
  • Eat it in the same meal as plant sources of iron (beans, lentils, spinach, fortified cereal) to absorb more of their iron.
  • Bell peppers, kiwi, citrus, strawberries and broccoli are rich everyday sources.

Good to know

  • Humans are one of the few animals that can’t make vitamin C, along with guinea pigs and some primates, bats and birds.
  • Scurvy, the deficiency disease, killed many sailors until citrus was found to prevent it in the 1700s.
  • Smokers need about 35 mg more a day, because smoking uses it up.
