Foods high in iron

Carries oxygen in your blood and muscles. The richest common sources per serving are chicken liver (cooked), chicken hearts (cooked) and bran flakes (fortified). The Daily Value is 18 mg a day for adults.

What iron does

Iron is the core of hemoglobin, which carries oxygen in your blood, and of myoglobin, which holds it in your muscles. Too little causes anemia, with tiredness, weakness, feeling cold and trouble concentrating. It’s the most common nutrient deficiency in the world.

How much you need

The Daily Value is 18 mg a day for adults. The safe upper limit for adults is 45 mg a day.

Tips

  • Iron from meat, fish and poultry (heme iron) is absorbed much better than iron from plants.
  • Eat vitamin C (citrus, bell peppers, tomatoes, strawberries) or a little meat or fish in the same meal to absorb much more of the iron in plant foods.
  • Acidic foods help too: lemon juice or tomato sauce with beans and greens. Cooking acidic food in a cast-iron pan even adds iron to it.
  • Tea, coffee and calcium block iron absorption, so have them between meals rather than with them.
  • Soaking, sprouting and fermenting beans and grains lowers the phytates that block iron.

What blocks it

  • Tea and coffee: Their polyphenols can cut the iron you absorb from a meal by half or more. Have them at least an hour before or after.
  • Milk, cheese and calcium supplements: Calcium blocks both plant and animal iron when eaten at the same time.
  • Bran and unsoaked beans or grains: Their phytates bind iron. Soaking, sprouting and fermenting free more of it.

Good to know

  • Women who have periods need more than twice as much as men: 18 mg a day versus 8 mg.
  • Too much iron is harmful. Don’t take iron supplements unless a blood test shows you need them.
  • Iron supplements are a common cause of poisoning in young children. Keep them out of reach.
