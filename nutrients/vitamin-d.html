Foods high in vitamin D (calciferol)

Helps you absorb calcium and keeps bones and muscles strong. The richest common sources per serving are rainbow trout (cooked), sockeye salmon (cooked) and swordfish (cooked). The Daily Value is 20 µg a day for adults.

What vitamin D (calciferol) does

Vitamin D lets your gut absorb calcium and phosphorus, so it’s essential for strong bones and teeth. It also supports your muscles and immune system. Your skin makes it from sunlight, which is why it’s called the sunshine vitamin.

How much you need

The Daily Value is 20 µg a day for adults. The safe upper limit for adults is 100 µg a day.

Tips

  • Few foods contain it naturally: mainly fatty fish, egg yolks and mushrooms exposed to UV light.
  • It’s fat-soluble, so take supplements with a meal that has some fat.
  • If you get little sun, have darker skin, or live far from the equator, ask a doctor whether you need a supplement.

What blocks it

  • Meals with no fat at all: Vitamin D is absorbed along with fat, so foods and supplements work best in a meal that has some.

Good to know

  • Far from the equator, the winter sun is too low for your skin to make much vitamin D.
  • Mushrooms make vitamin D in UV light, much like your skin does.
  • Too much from supplements can raise calcium to harmful levels. The upper limit for adults is 100 µg (4,000 IU) a day.
