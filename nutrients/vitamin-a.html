Foods high in vitamin A (all forms)

Needed for seeing in dim light, a strong immune system and healthy skin. The richest common sources per serving are beef liver (cooked), chicken liver (cooked) and carrot juice. The Daily Value is 900 µg a day for adults.

What vitamin A (all forms) does

Vitamin A forms the light-sensitive pigment in your eyes that lets you see in dim light. It also keeps the surfaces of your eyes, airways and gut healthy, so they can keep germs out. Animal foods contain ready-made vitamin A (retinol); orange and dark-green plants contain beta-carotene, which your body turns into vitamin A as needed.

How much you need

The Daily Value is 900 µg a day for adults. The safe upper limit for adults is 3000 µg a day.

Tips

  • It’s fat-soluble: eat it with a little fat for better absorption.
  • Cooking and chopping carrots, sweet potatoes and greens releases more of their beta-carotene.
  • Liver is extremely high in vitamin A. Keep portions small, especially during pregnancy, when too much can harm the baby.

What blocks it

  • Meals with no fat at all: Vitamin A and beta-carotene dissolve in fat. A little oil, nuts or avocado in the meal helps you absorb much more of them.

Good to know

  • Trouble seeing at night is an early sign of too little. Worldwide, vitamin A deficiency is a leading cause of preventable blindness in children.
  • Beta-carotene from food can’t cause a vitamin A overdose, because your body converts only what it needs. Eating lots of it can tint your skin slightly orange, which is harmless.
