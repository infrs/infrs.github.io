Foods high in vitamin B9 (folate)

Needed to make DNA and red blood cells; especially important before and during pregnancy. The richest common sources per serving are chicken liver (cooked), toasted oat cereal (cheerios, fortified) and edamame. The Daily Value is 400 µg a day for adults.

What vitamin B9 (folate) does

Folate helps make and repair DNA, so it’s vital for cells that divide quickly, such as red blood cells and a growing baby’s cells. Getting enough before and in early pregnancy greatly lowers the risk of neural tube defects such as spina bifida.

How much you need

The Daily Value is 400 µg a day for adults. The safe upper limit for adults is 1000 µg a day from supplements and fortified foods.

Tips

  • Leafy greens, legumes, asparagus and avocado are rich natural sources.
  • Boiling in lots of water washes folate out, so steaming or microwaving keeps more.
  • Anyone who could become pregnant is advised to take 400 µg of folic acid a day, on top of folate from food.

What blocks it

  • Alcohol: It lowers how much folate you absorb and makes your body lose more of it.

Good to know

  • Its name comes from the Latin "folium", leaf, because it was first found in spinach leaves.
  • Folic acid, the form in supplements and fortified foods, is absorbed better than the folate in food. Amounts here are adjusted for that.
