Foods high in vitamin B3 (niacin)

Supports energy use, skin, digestion and the nervous system. The richest common sources per serving are energy drink, yellowfin tuna (cooked) and chicken breast (pan-fried, no breading). The Daily Value is 16 mg a day for adults.

What vitamin B3 (niacin) does

Niacin is part of NAD, a coenzyme used by more than 400 enzymes: for turning food into energy, making fats and cholesterol, and repairing DNA.

How much you need

The Daily Value is 16 mg a day for adults. The safe upper limit for adults is 35 mg a day from supplements and fortified foods.

Tips

  • Meat, fish, poultry, peanuts and enriched grains are rich sources.
  • Your body can also make niacin from tryptophan, an amino acid in protein-rich foods.

Good to know

  • Pellagra, a deficiency that causes rashes, diarrhea and confusion, was common where corn was the staple, because the niacin in corn is hard to absorb. Soaking corn in lime water, as in making tortillas, releases it.
  • High-dose niacin supplements can cause skin flushing and liver problems. Don’t take them without medical advice.
